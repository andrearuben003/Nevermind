\section{Team meetings and Deadlines}
\subsection{Week 1}
\subsubsection{October 6 team meeting}
The team agreed upon the core idea and goal of the game: collecting memories and use them to solve puzzles. \par
Discussed main game mechanics: the team is split between memories being physical objects or items that stay in an intentory of sort (or maybe a hybrid). \par
Discussed the way memories can be remebered and forgotten: the team is undecided between a fixed place or everywhere (this also depends on the way memories work, so that has to be decided first). \par
Discussed upon the idea of \textit{levels}: the team is split between a metroidvania style, based more on exploration, and a hardcore puzzle style, based more on mechanical skill and puzzle solving. \par
Sketched some potential ideas for memories: meterials (like bricks), gravity, yourself (become a ghost), light, dark, time (may be used to pause the game), enemies. Sketched some more ideas like collectibles, bosses (that might unlock memory 'slots'), 'bad' memories (debuffs forced in some cases), frail memories (they can break).
\subsubsection{October 13 deadline}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bring a prototype based on the way memories work: either them being physical objects or items in an inventory (everyone)
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Week 2}
\subsubsection{October 13 team meeting}
Prototypes from everyone have been seen or tested to discuss the game design. After playtests and discussions, the team agreed upon:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Making levels with more screens to keep a theme (like Celeste)
    \item Making memories physical objects that can be stored in an inventory or dropped onto the ground
    \item Letting the player remember and forget everywhere
    \item The number of memory slots (how many memories can be held at one): the player starts with 1 or 2 and defeating bosses unlocks more slots (maximum of 5 to 7)
\end{itemize}
Due to the nature of the game, memory classes have been designed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Permanent: typically given at the end of a level, they are permanent upgrades (like double jump)
    \item Semi-permanent: they only stay with the player during a level
    \item Frail: they can break if the player touches some specific objects (like in Portal)
\end{itemize}
Sketched some more ideas for memories: hands (ability to grab items), weapons? (can be too violent), object properties? (maybe better to stick with the objects themselves), rubber tile/spring (forces you to bounce/jump). \par
Sketched ideas for bosses: forces you memories and you have to adapt; gives you 'bad' memories. \par
The idea of collectibles remains viable, it will be discussed later on. \par
Andrea Ruben has shown the team his drawings and sketches for a potential game style.
\subsubsection{October 20 deadline}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sketch levels (even on paper) to test for the viability of some specific memories. Try to test as many as possible with as many ideas as possible (everyone)
\end{itemize}
